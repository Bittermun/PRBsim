**Subject**: Technical Research Inquiry: Post-Wildfire Coal Seam Combustion Records (2024 Remington Fire Perimeter)

**Dear Coordinators and Specialists**,

I am conducting an independent geospatial research project evaluating the spatial and temporal interaction between surface vegetation fires and shallow coal seam combustion in the Powder River Basin. Specifically, my study focuses on the 196,368-acre perimeter of the **Remington Wildfire** (Unique Fire ID: `2024-WYSHX-240442`, August–September 2024).

To ensure that spatial analysis relies strictly on empirically verified records rather than speculative assumptions, I am requesting information regarding:

1. **Active Vent & Ground Hotspot Inspections**:
   - Are there records, GIS points, or field inspection logs of coal outcrops, smoldering fissures, or ground-level carbon monoxide/sulfur vents identified within or adjacent to the Remington Fire perimeter during or following containment?
   - If present, what measurement methods were used (e.g., Forward Looking Infrared [FLIR], handheld thermal radiometers, multi-gas sniffers)?

2. **Pre-Fire Historical Baseline**:
   - Does your agency maintain an inventory of known active or historic coal seam fires (natural or legacy AML sites) within Rosebud, Powder River, Big Horn (MT), or Sheridan/Campbell (WY) counties that predated August 2024?

3. **Negative Survey Footprints**:
   - Were specific aerial thermal or ground reconnaissance surveys conducted post-containment that detected *no* persistent combustion? If so, what were the spatial boundaries and instrument thermal sensitivity thresholds?

**Format Preference**: Shapefile (.shp), GeoJSON, or tabular logs with coordinate references (WGS84 / NAD83) and observation dates.

Thank you very much for your time and public service.

Sincerely,  
*Independent Researcher*
